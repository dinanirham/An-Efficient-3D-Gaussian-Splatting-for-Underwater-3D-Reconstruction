# 3.8.3 Treatment of Unresolved and Undetermined Effects

Section 3.6.5 defines the labels. This subsection states the reporting rules
that follow from them, which are what a reader of Chapter IV should be able to
hold the text to.

**An unresolved effect is reported, with its value, and is never described as
absent.** The value and its standard error appear in the table; the label
appears beside it; and no sentence in Chapter IV converts "not separable from
repeat dispersion" into "no effect". This rule has teeth in one direction in
particular: every mechanism in this study has at least one fidelity effect that
fails to resolve, and describing those as free would systematically flatter the
mechanisms.

**An undetermined quantity is computed, tabulated and not interpreted.** The
peak signal-to-noise interactions are the case. Omitting them would leave a
reader to assume they were never computed; interpreting them would violate a
rule registered before the analysis. They are therefore printed with the label
and no discussion, and Section 4.6.1 gives the arithmetic that justifies the
label along with the observation that their signs flip between scenes — which
is corroborating evidence for the resolution argument rather than a separate
finding.

**A single-scene result is described as a single-scene result.** Where an
effect resolves on one scene of four, Chapter IV names the scene and does not
generalise. This is the structural defence against multiplicity described in
Section 3.8.2, and it is checkable: any sentence in Chapter IV claiming a
mechanism property should be traceable to an effect resolving consistently
across scenes.

**A post-hoc statement is labelled where it appears.** Not in a footnote and
not in a terminal caveat, because a reader forms a view at the point of
reading. Section 4.7.4 is the largest instance — the study's most detailed
characterisation of the medium failure is entirely post-hoc and says so in its
first sentence.

## Negative and inconvenient results

**A pre-registered prediction that fails is reported in the body, at the point
where the prediction was made, and is not reclassified as exploratory.** The
campaign contains two: the additivity prediction for one mechanism pair, and
the identifiability account that motivated this study's own intervention.
Section 3.6.4 records both as registered, and Chapter IV reports both as
failed.

The second of these is a negative result about a component this study
contributed rather than inherited. Reporting it prominently is not
self-flagellation; it is the only treatment that leaves the rest of the chapter
credible. A thesis that reports its inherited mechanisms' failures and not its
own invites the reader to discount everything.

**A withdrawn account is withdrawn, not qualified.** Where a registered
explanation fails its own stated test, this thesis removes it rather than
retaining it with caveats, and says what is left: in the case of the medium
coupling, a rate and a boundary alignment that are established, and a mechanism
that is not.

## What this leaves Chapter V able to claim

The cumulative effect of these rules is that Chapter IV establishes fewer
mechanisms than a conventionally reported campaign of the same size would
appear to. What it establishes instead is measurements with stated resolution,
and the distinction between the two is the thing the conclusions chapter must
respect. Section 5.2 states contributions at evidence level for this reason: a
contribution is what the campaign measured, not what it can be made to suggest.

## Review log

**Domain Researcher** — The draft stated the rules without saying which
direction each failure would bias. The unresolved-as-absent misreading is
uniformly favourable to the mechanisms under test, and naming that is what
turns a convention into a safeguard. → *applied*. Second finding: the treatment
of single-scene results was not connected to the multiplicity discussion of
Section 3.8.2, where it functions as the defence. → *applied*.

**Supervisor** — The draft treated the two failed predictions symmetrically.
One is a failure of an inherited claim and the other of this study's own
contribution, and the second is the one whose reporting demonstrates that the
rules are real. → *applied*, with the argument that reporting only inherited
failures would invite a reader to discount everything. Devil's advocate: do
these rules leave the thesis with too little to claim? They leave it with what
it measured, which Section 5.2 is written to state — and that is a smaller set
than a conventionally reported campaign would present.

**Journal Reviewer** — The rules were given without saying how a reader could
verify compliance. → *applied*: the single-scene rule is stated as checkable,
with what to check. Second: "withdrawn, not qualified" appeared in Section
3.6.4 and here without the two being cross-referenced. → *applied*.
