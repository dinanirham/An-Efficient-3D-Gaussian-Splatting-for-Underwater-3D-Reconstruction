# 4.9.3 Discussion

The interesting thing about this contrast is not its own numbers but what it
sits beside. It reduces the primitive population by three to five times,
continuously and without any scheduled event, and the medium model survives in
every one of its twelve runs. Spatial reorganisation reduces the population by
fourteen to twenty-five times, at two scheduled events, and the medium model
fails in five of twelve.

Read together, the two are consistent with the descriptive account of Section
4.7.4: that what matters is the size of the cut and the level the attenuation
reaches after it, rather than the size of the resulting representation. A
mechanism that removes primitives gradually never presents the medium model
with a discontinuity to absorb, and the coefficients are never driven far
enough in 200 steps to approach zero.

**This is an observation consistent with that account, and it is not a test of
it.** The two mechanisms differ in more than the abruptness of their reduction:
in how much they remove, in when, in what they leave behind, and in whether a
medium-only optimisation interval follows. Nothing in this campaign varies
abruptness while holding the rest fixed. A reader should take the comparison as
one more thing the descriptive account accommodates, and not as evidence for
it, because an account that accommodates observations it was constructed from
gains nothing by accommodating one more.

The comparison does, however, sharpen the question Section 4.7.5 leaves open.
If a continuous reduction of three to five times leaves the medium intact and a
two-step reduction of fourteen to twenty-five times does not, the experiment
that would separate the candidate causes is a continuous reduction to the same
final population — around 130 000 primitives — with no event and no
medium-only interval. That configuration does not exist in this campaign and
would answer more than the single missing cell named in Section 4.7.5. Both are
carried to Section 5.4.

As a practical option this mechanism occupies a distinct position. It is the
only configuration here that reduces the population without a scheduled
intervention, its perceptual cost is the smallest of any mechanism that resolves
at all, and it leaves the medium model untouched on every run. What it does not
offer is magnitude: three to five times against fourteen to twenty-five for
spatial reorganisation and nine to eighteen for deterministic initialisation,
and against the visible population a reduction of only one and a half to two
and a half times. It is the conservative option, and it is on the
count-against-perceptual-error front on one scene of four (Section 4.6.6).

One caution applies to any use of this result. The mechanism is measured here
against the unmodified configuration and never against the published reference,
so its standing relative to published SeaSplat is not established by this
campaign. That is a consequence of it being a supplementary contrast rather
than a factor, and it is stated rather than worked around.
