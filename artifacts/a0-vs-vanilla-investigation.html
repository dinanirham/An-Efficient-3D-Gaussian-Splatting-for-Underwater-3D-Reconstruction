Why A0 builds a sixth of the model

A0 converges to ~635k primitives where vanilla SeaSplat reaches 4,462,668 on the same scene. Four hypotheses, three of them wrong, and the measurement that finally localised the cause to a single quantity.

Current state

Every component that can be compared statically is identical — rasterizer forward and backward, densification constants, upstream commit, and the resolved configuration (111 of 113 shared keys equal). The runs still diverge sixfold.

The event-by-event comparison localised it to one quantity that diverges before any other: the fraction of primitives clearing densify_grad_threshold. Ours decays to 0.2%; the reference holds 3–5%. Three variants bracket the cause, and the current fix (CD-23) is implemented but not yet measured.

The measurement that localised it

The reference holds 3–5% for the whole run. Ours decays monotonically. Cumulatively over 144 events: clone 0.109×, split 0.083×, prune 0.090× — we do less of everything, in proportion, because fewer primitives ever clear the threshold.

Why no endpoint comparison could have found this. Vanilla grows 6.7× between iterations 5000 and 15000, which across 100 densification events is only ~1.9% growth apiece. A deficit that small is invisible on a trajectory plot and says nothing about which of clone, split or prune differs. It only becomes legible when the events are counted separately and aligned.

The bracket

Three variants, same scene, same seed, same data:

Densification buffer receivesWhereFinal primitives
image losses onlyours, before CD-22743,457

CD-22 made it worse, and that is the informative part. Adding alpha and depth together produced fewer primitives than image alone, so the depth term is not neutral in that buffer — it cancels. Upstream never has it there: its depth pass allocates its own gradient buffer.

CD-23 gives alpha its own probe, [0,1,0] — channel 1 accumulates 1 − Π(1−aᵢ) exactly as before, channel 0 carries no depth, so only alpha reaches the shared buffer. Three rasterizations per iteration instead of two. That cost is real and lands in the wall clock this study reports, which is why it sits behind separate_alpha_probe and is recorded in every manifest rather than compiled in.

Why it matters

The factorial stays internally consistent — all eight cells share one renderer — but three things break while A0 is not the baseline:

  1. n_bud would be derived from the wrong number. From 635k the budget lands near 400k; a faithful SeaSplat converges past 4.4M.
  2. Two cells could collapse. A budget that fails to bind makes A4 ≡ A1 and A7 ≡ A5, and a null interaction measured in that state looks exactly like a finding.
  3. No external comparison holds against SeaSplat's published table.

The quality result, which is not yet a result. At 6× fewer primitives our A0 scored 30.268 dB test PSNR against the reference's 30.375 — a tenth of a dB. If that survives proper measurement it is a larger, simpler efficiency lever than anything M1–M3 offers. But it rests on one seed, one scene and a three-image test set whose mean is dragged by a single 24.5 dB frame. It is a promising observation, not a finding.

Open items

  • CD-23 is unmeasured. Target at iteration 14900: over_grad near 3% and primitives in the millions. This is the fourth hypothesis; the first three were wrong, and the difference is that this one comes from a measured bracket rather than from reading code.
  • If it still decays, something else feeds that buffer differently, and the next step is logging per-loss-group gradient contributions directly instead of inferring them.
  • A 4.6% accounting gap. diagnostics.csv reported 743,457 at iteration 30000 where the 48.2 MB PLY implies ~709k and the 39 MB compressed artifact ~696k. The two artifacts agree with each other and disagree with the log.
  • Cost is re-derived and lower than feared. Vanilla ran 30,000 iterations at 4.46M primitives in ~51 minutes wall clock. An earlier version of this document warned the corrected campaign would be far more expensive; that was wrong. Storage rises, not time.

What the instrumentation is worth keeping for

Three checks now exist that would have caught this class of defect immediately, and none of them existed when the merge was written:

  • T7 — asserts the gradient's destination, in both directions: non-zero with a shared buffer, untouched without one.
  • T8 — asserts the alpha-only probe matches the combined probe's alpha to 1e-6 and that its depth channel is identically zero.
  • The per-event breakdown — clone, split and each prune reason counted separately, on both implementations, aligned event by event.

Every acceptance check written before this investigation inspected a returned tensor. Alpha's value was correct throughout; what was wrong was where its gradient went. That is the lesson worth carrying into the write-up.
