## 🟢 The one genuinely good interaction in the branch set

**A2 partially restores the removal mechanism A1 destroyed.**

- A1 disables `densify_and_prune`, and with it the `min_opacity = 0.005` prune, so
  SeaSplat's `L_op` (λ = 0.01) suppresses backscatter-only Gaussians to `α ≈ 0` with nothing
  to remove them (`a1.md` A1-1).
- A2's importance `opacity × scale` reads post-`L_op` opacity, so it ranks exactly those
  Gaussians lowest and prunes them first (`a2.md` A2-3).

The mechanism A1 broke and the mechanism A2 supplies are, by coincidence, the same mechanism.
**This should be stated and measured deliberately** — it is the most defensible efficiency
result available from these branches, and at present it is undocumented and untested.

### But the restoration is partial, in two specific ways

| Gap | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Coverage in time** | Baseline prunes from iteration 500, every 100 iterations. A2 prunes from **15 500**, every 500. On A4 there is a **15 500-iteration window with no population management whatsoever** — no densification, no α-prune, no opacity reset, no reorg. `L_op` accumulates suppressed Gaussians for more than half the run with no executor. |
| **Coverage in criterion** | The baseline prune is a **threshold** (`α < 0.005` → remove, unconditionally). A2 is a **budget** (remove only enough to reach `max_gaussians`). If the population is under budget, α ≈ 0 Gaussians survive to the final `.ply` no matter how many there are. |

## 🔴 C4-1 — A4 may be behaviourally identical to A1

This is the decisive structural conflict, and it follows from combining two facts already
established:

1. A1 freezes the population: with densification off, **`N` can never increase.**
2. A2 is a no-op whenever `N ≤ max_gaussians` (`reorganize_gaussians` returns `0` early).

Therefore on A4, `N(t)` is monotonically non-increasing and starts at `N_init`, the point
count `roma_init.py` produced. So:

> **If `N_init ≤ 800 000`, A2 never fires at any iteration, and
> `feature/a4-init-reorg` is behaviourally identical to `feature/a1-deterministic-init`.**

Unlike the baseline branch A2 — where densification can push the count above budget before
15 000 — on A4 there is no mechanism that can ever bring `N` *up* to the threshold. The
budget is either binding at iteration 15 500 or binding never.

`N_init` is controlled by `roma_init.py`'s `voxel = 0.001`, `matches = 5000`, `nns = 3` and
`num_refs = -1`, and is **recorded nowhere** — `docs/` is empty and the dense `.ply` is not
versioned (`a1.md` A1-6). **One number decides whether one of the eight factorial cells
exists.** See `refit_recommendations.md` R-1.

## 🟡 C4-2 — A2's stated rationale is false on this branch

The A2 block carries the comment:

> *"Guarantees budget cap is maintained even after densification regrows the population."*

On A4, densification is disabled. There is no regrowth. The comment describes A2's role on
its own branch and was carried into the combination unchanged. Harmless at runtime, but it
documents an interaction that does not occur and obscures the one that does (the `L_op`
interaction above).

## 🟡 C4-3 — The factorial's additivity assumption is violated here

The README frames A0–A7 as a 2³ factorial over three "independent" mechanisms. A1 and A2 are
**not independent**: both act on the same state variable, the Gaussian population `N`. A1
sets its initial value and freezes its dynamics; A2 caps it. Their effects compose
multiplicatively through a shared variable, not additively.

This does not invalidate the design — a full factorial is precisely the right instrument for
*detecting* interaction — but it means **the A1×A2 interaction term is expected to be large
and must be reported, not averaged away.** Reporting A4 as "A1's effect plus A2's effect"
would be wrong. See `validation_alignment.md`.
