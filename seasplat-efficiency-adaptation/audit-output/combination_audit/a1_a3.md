## 🔴 C5-1 — The compounding defect: unpruned α ≈ 0 Gaussians meet opacity quantization

Two individually-moderate findings combine into the sharpest correctness risk in the audit.

| Ingredient | Source |
|---|---|
| A1 disables `densify_and_prune`, so the `min_opacity = 0.005` prune never runs; SeaSplat's `L_op` suppresses backscatter-only Gaussians to `α ≈ 0` but **nothing removes them**. They are written to the `.ply`. | `a1.md` A1-1 |
| A3 includes `opacity` in `to_quantize`, which compact3d explicitly excludes `[compact3d §3]`. | `a3.md` A3-3 |

**The interaction.** On A1-derived branches the saved model contains an unusually large,
unusually concentrated mass of Gaussians at the extreme low end of the opacity logit
distribution — a population that on every other branch would have been pruned away. K-means
allocates centroids by density, so this mass **pulls centroids into the low-opacity tail**
and, more importantly, the Gaussians nearest the α ≈ 0.005 visibility boundary get snapped to
whichever centroid is closest — which may sit above the boundary.

The result is that **quantization can re-materialize precisely the floaters SeaSplat's
opacity prior was designed to suppress**, in the one branch family where no prune exists to
catch them afterwards. Neither ingredient is severe alone. Together they undo a load-bearing
piece of SeaSplat's underwater model, and they do it in the *final artifact*, after all
optimization has ended.

Two properties make this hard to detect:

1. **Post-hoc means no recovery.** Under compact3d's QAT the photometric loss would push
   re-materialized Gaussians back down. Here there is no gradient step left.
2. **PSNR against a raw underwater GT under-reports it.** Re-materialized floaters appear in
   the *restored* image `Ĵ` and in backscatter-dominated regions; the reported metric
   compares the *rendered underwater* image to the raw GT (`validation_alignment.md`), where
   the damage is partly masked by the medium model itself.

**Mitigation is cheap:** remove `'opacity'` from `to_quantize`. It is 1 of 14 floats, so the
compression cost is ~2 bytes per 20 (`a3.md` A3-7 arithmetic), and it restores fidelity to
compact3d. This is the single best cost-to-benefit fix in the audit (`refit_recommendations.md` R-2).

## 🔴 C5-2 — Worst compression on the largest model

A5 combines:

- the **largest** Gaussian count of any branch — dense RoMa init, never pruned, never reset;
- A3's ≈ 2.8× structural compression ceiling, of which **protected `xyz` is 60% of the
  residual** (`a3.md` A3-7).

Since `xyz` is 12 of the 20 post-quantization bytes and A1 maximizes the number of `xyz`
entries, **A1 inflates exactly the term A3 cannot compress.** compact3d identified this
dependency directly — after quantization, position dominates memory, so the only remaining
lever is *fewer Gaussians* `[compact3d §3]` — and A5 pushes that lever in the wrong direction.

If the thesis reports a compression ratio for A5, it will be the least favourable in the set,
and for a structural reason that is predictable in advance rather than an empirical surprise.

## 🟡 C5-3 — `f_dc` is doubly perturbed

A1 seeds `_features_dc` from RoMa-sampled source-image colours (or, on the fallback path,
uniform grey 0.5 — `a1.md` A1-8). A3 then snaps `f_dc` to 256 centroids post-hoc, with no
gradient step to let `L_gw` (grey-world, λ = 0.1) and `L_sat` (λ = 2.0) rebalance
(`a3.md` A3-2).

SeaSplat's colour losses operate on **aggregate statistics** of the restored image `Ĵ`, so a
quantization-induced shift in the mean is exactly the kind of error they were tuned to
prevent — and exactly the kind PSNR against a raw underwater GT will barely register. This
affects **restoration quality**, which is SeaSplat's actual contribution, more than it
affects the reported number.

compounding effect that A7 partially masks.
