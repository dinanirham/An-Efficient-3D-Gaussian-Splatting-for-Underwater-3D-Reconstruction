## Internal claims that need a caveat when cited

| Claim | Caveat |
|---|---|
| "significantly accelerates both training and rendering while reducing peak memory" `[paper §6.1]` | **Outdoor only.** Indoors, training is 9% *slower* (27 m 02 s vs 24 m 41 s) at parity memory (2.77 vs 2.75 GB) `[paper Tab. 2]`. Rendering wins hold in both. |
| "comparable to 3DGS, despite employing only a fraction (7× fewer) of the Gaussians" `[paper §6.1]` | Carried by PSNR/SSIM. **LPIPS is slightly worse than 3DGS on 2 of 3 datasets** (0.217 vs 0.216; 0.202 vs 0.176; 0.253 vs 0.244) `[paper Tab. 1]`. |
| Densification improves quality `[paper Tab. 3]` | **PSNR is flat** (27.47 → 27.47 → 27.54). Gains are in SSIM/LPIPS, and the paper itself notes "a high correlation between the number of Gaussians and the LPIPS metric" — which is close to tautological. The *distribution* claim rests on Figs. 1/2/12 and Tab. 5. |
| Mid-point depth is the right formulation `[paper §4.1, App. D]` | Tab. 4 shows **Center 27.57 vs Mid 27.54** — a wash. The choice is justified on point-cloud quality and future normal estimation, **not** on rendering metrics. Appendix D's derivation supports a claim Table 4 does not make. |
| Mini-Splatting-C beats dedicated compression methods `[paper §6.1, Fig. 9]` | Baselines "collected from their respective papers" — **not re-run on the same hardware or protocol**. Plus the undocumented voxel dedup (D-10) and the 8-bit disk round-trip (D-14). |
| "Mini-Splatting-D even surpasses … Zip-NeRF [3] in terms of SSIM and LPIPS" `[paper §6.1]` | True as stated, and the paper is careful to scope it to SSIM/LPIPS. Zip-NeRF still leads on PSNR by **1.03 dB** (28.54 vs 27.51) `[paper Tab. 1]`. |
